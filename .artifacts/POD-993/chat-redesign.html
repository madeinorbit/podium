Transcript chat view

The Claude Design handoff, built. Shots are the real stylesheet against the real component DOM, in both themes and at both widths — dark and light are one implementation and differ only in which tokens the theme resolves.

The breath

The working mark is a face-on ring of dots whose radius undulates — two travelling waves around the circumference, three lanes deep, brightest where a lobe swells. It does not spin and it does not pulse: there is no frame you could point at as the beat, which is what lets it be watched for a minute without becoming irritating. 22px at the end of the feed, 14px and one lane on the tab that leads to it, so a background chat says it is working with the same mark rather than a second, differently shaped one.

Painted on one canvas from a single shared rAF loop, because the per-dot phase makes it 78 animated nodes in CSS. Under prefers-reduced-motion it paints one frame and never asks for another.

The human's turn is a card, on the right

Side is the cheapest statement of who spoke: no colour, no label, no vertical space. The agent keeps the full column and the flat ground — only one voice gets a card. The card is a real surface now rather than a tint: the theme's one-step-off-the-sheet ground, a hairline rim and a single lit pixel along its top edge, with the corner that points at the speaker left square. Below ~520px of column it drops the indent and takes the width. ANSWER is the one voice label left, because it is where a turn lands and what a reader scans back for.

The foot: two registers, one curve

The clock and the buttons are not the same kind of thing and no longer fade as one. The clock is information — you should be able to answer "when was this?" without hunting with a mouse — so it rests at 40% and comes to full ink on hover. The buttons rest at nothing, two pixels low, and rise into place under the pointer: a transcript showing a copy and a quote glyph under every paragraph is a transcript wearing its toolbar. 280ms on one curve, both directions, both parts.

The pinned brief left the column

This was position: sticky on the row itself, plus a JS pass writing a translate onto the outgoing brief on every scroll frame so two consecutive ones could hand off. It worked, and it cost the thing a transcript can least afford: the row was still in the flow, so pinning and unpinning changed the height of the document under the reader's eyes.

The brief in the feed is now only ever a brief — whole, no clamp, no toggle, no transform. The pinned state is a separate shelf drawn over the column: same geometry and ink, clamped to two lines behind a mask, with Show full one click away on the right. It can appear and leave without moving a single row, and it costs one thing — occlusion — which it pays for with a blur and a rim so what is underneath reads as underneath.

Podium's mail is one object per burst

Each frame used to be its own row with its own header, so a turn that received three notes spent three rows saying almost the same thing before the answer got a word in. The whole burst is one quiet tinted line now — "3 notes from Podium", with the issues they came from — which opens into a real mail list: sender in a fixed column, subject and time on one baseline, two lines of body. A frame that asks a question or requests a reply opens the card on arrival, because that is the one kind of mail with a consequence. Nothing is lost: clicking an item opens that frame's full markdown, refs and code live.

The run of tool calls

Unchanged in structure and retuned to the handoff's numbers: one row with a fanned deck behind it standing for the calls it folds, the count in a bordered pill, the elapsed figure in machine voice, and the whole stack unfolding in place. It carries its own margin now — wider than the feed's beat — because it is a solid in the document and prose has to clear its edges.

The prompt well

One field across the full column with its controls on the floor below the text, so a wide pane no longer gives up ~90px on every line to a cluster that needs the last one. Recessed rather than outlined: a floor, a rim, a shadow falling in over the top edge and a lit pixel along the bottom. The ⌘/ to focus hint sits on the well while the field is unfocused and empty — so it can never land on your own words — fading in over 700ms and out in 120ms.

Rhythm

The design separates two questions the old spacing conflated. The beat is what sits between two rows of one turn (12px); the seam is what sits between turns (21px, down from 60). The objects that are not prose carry their own margin on top of the beat. The column also gets a measure back — 888px, centred, expressed as the scroller's own padding so every voice obeys it and below ~950px it collapses to exactly the flat 32px gutter it replaces.

The two deviations, on purpose

The handoff sets its mono micro register at 9.5, 10 and 10.5px. This app has a stated type floor at 10.5px with a test behind it, so all three land on the floor — half a point of hierarchy inside a register nobody resolves by size, traded for the smallest text in the busiest surface staying legible.

The handoff's working row ends in an Esc stop control. Not built: our interrupt is a double-Escape and only in headless mode, so the label would have advertised a keybinding that does not exist. Filed rather than faked.
