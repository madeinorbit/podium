Session presence — the four states of the header strip

POD-1535. ADR 7's rooms had a transport and no surface; this is the surface. The strip sits in the session header's right cluster and answers one question: who else is on this session. The whole design turns on the last two cases below being different.

1 · Two other people, one of them reading the terminal

Neutral chips, not a hue per person: The Reserved Hues Rule keeps identity out of the signal colours, and this header is already tinted by the issue-colour channel. Identity reads from the mono token. Hovering names them and says what each is looking at.

2 · An agent is watching too, and more people than fit

An agent carries ADR 3 D17's attribution pair, so the strip can say whose agent is watching without a second identity concept. Past three, the rest collapse to +N.

3 · Only you — a fact, and stated as one

refactor the feed cursor ~/src/podium opus 5 · high ChatNative

The server told us the room is empty. Tooltip: “Only you are here.”

4 · We do not know — and this is not the same as “nobody”

refactor the feed cursor ~/src/podium opus 5 · high ChatNative

Disconnected, not joined yet, or the join was refused — the protocol answers all of those with one reason-free frame, on purpose. The glyph drops to Faint ink and the tooltip says “Presence unavailable”. Never a count, never the word “nobody”.

This distinction is carried by the TYPE, not by a convention: PresenceRoomView has no members array on its unknown arm, so no component can map over an empty list and paint “alone”. Mutating that arm to { status: 'present', members: [] } turns three tests red.
