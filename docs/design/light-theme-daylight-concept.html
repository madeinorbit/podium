Podium · Superade · light variant · v2

Daylight

A light theme that keeps every idea the dark one is built on — carved depth, one yellow signal, tinted issue surfaces — by mirroring the mechanics instead of inverting the hexes.

What changed in v2. The dark top bar is gone. And the palette stopped being hand-picked: I measured Linear, Cursor and Conductor's light UIs, found three specific things my first pass got wrong, and rebuilt the neutrals as generated values. Everything below is downstream of that measurement.

What the references actually do

Three numbers explain why v1 felt simple.

I pulled the live CSS custom properties off cursor.com and conductor.build and read Linear's own write-up on their theme system, then converted everything to OKLCH so the values are comparable. Three patterns show up in all of them, and v1 broke all three.

1 · They are neutral, not tinted.

Linear says it outright — they got a "neutral and timeless appearance" by limiting blue chrome in the light theme's color calculations. Cursor and Conductor both go faintly warm. A grey with obvious hue reads as generated; a grey with a whisper of it reads as chosen. v1's blue-grey was the single loudest reason it felt ordinary.

2 · They separate with line, not tone.

Cursor's whole app lives in 4.2 L-points. Conductor's panels are all white with one hairline between them. Heavy tonal tiering is a dark-mode idiom — on a dark ground you have room below you and no hairline reads cleanly. v1 ported seven tiers spanning 10.9 points straight across, which is what made it feel grey and heavy rather than light.

3 · Their greys are one ink at varying alpha.

Cursor doesn't define grey hexes at all. Every border and secondary text is color-mix(in oklab, #26251e N%, transparent) — 2.5%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 60% of the same ink. That is why it coheres: everything on screen is literally the same color. Linear reduced two whole themes to three variables (base, accent, contrast) in LCH for the same reason. v1 hand-picked six ink hexes, and they drifted.

Decision one · the foundation

One ink. And it was already in the palette.

Generate the ink instead of picking it: take the dark theme's navy hue (264°), the chroma a brand-carrying ink can hold (0.035), and the lightness a near-black needs (20%). That is oklch(0.20 0.035 264) — which resolves to #0e1626.

#0e1626 is Rail Navy. It is already in DESIGN.md, where it is a surface in the dark theme. The mirror turns out to be literal: a navy the dark theme paints panels with becomes the ink the light theme writes with. Nothing was invented — and every screen is quietly navy without a single navy surface, which is what carries the brand now that the dark top bar is gone.

Every neutral in the theme is that one ink at an alpha. Not six greys that happen to look related — one color, nine strengths.

Decision two

The ramp mirrors — and compresses.

In the dark theme the chassis is the darkest thing and every surface steps up toward the light: bar → engraved → tabstrip → rail → panel → chip. Daylight keeps that sentence exactly and runs it the other way — but across 6.2 L-points instead of 10.9, and at a quarter of v1's chroma. Same seven tiers, half the shout.

Every surface is oklch(L C 264) with C between 0.006 and 0 — inside the band the references live in. The field is off-white at 97.4%, not the mid-grey v1 used; pure white is spent only on the raised tier, so "whitest" keeps meaning "closest to you."

Compressing the tones means hairlines have to do more work — which is exactly the trade the references make. It also raises the stakes on the next decision: with the tiers this close, the engraved groove is no longer a supporting cue. It is the depth.

Decision three

Carve in navy. Never in black, never upward.

The Carved Rule is the signature: depth goes inward, resting surfaces cast nothing, drop shadows belong only to things that will disappear. It survives — but the shadow has to become a navy groove at low alpha, not black at 0.85.

This is not a hypothetical port problem. --shadow-engraved is declared constant across presets in index.css today, so the light theme currently paints rgb(0 0 0 / 0.85) onto a near-white field. Black on paper isn't depth, it's dirt. I rendered it against the compressed ramp at 22 / 26 / 32% — 26% is the one that reads as machined, and 32% bands.

Decision four · confirmed

Yellow keeps the crown.

DESIGN.md currently hands primary to Royal Blue in light. That call gets reversed: a blue-primary light theme is Linear, Height and Notion, and it surrenders the one thing nobody else has. The premise doesn't hold either — the rule was never yellow text, it's Rail Navy ink on a yellow fill, which measures 11.1:1.

This also becomes the theme's whole chromatic point of view. With the neutrals down at chroma 0.006 and the ink carrying the brand, Superade Yellow is the only saturated color on a resting screen — apart from the issue tints, which are the operator's own doing. One hue at rest. That is the restraint the references get from being monochrome, and Podium gets it while keeping a signal color.

Decision five

Halve every tint. Ease the hairlines.

The issue-color channel is the other signature, and its percentages are tuned for navy. A hue mixed into a dark base is absorbed; mixed into a light one it saturates about twice as fast. Ported as-is, a selected row at 28% becomes a flat fill in all but name — which the Tint-Never-Fill Rule exists to prevent.

Correction from implementation. This section first claimed hairlines had to go up, because a translucent hue on a light ground loses its edge. I asserted that without testing it, and it is wrong — rendered against both grounds, a mid-lightness palette hue reads more strongly on paper than on navy, not less. Hairlines ease slightly down instead (×0.85), which mostly reins in the loud 70–80% values.

The result

The shell, both ways.

The command header is now the deepest paper tier rather than navy — a bezel by tone, the way Cursor's chrome sits against its editor. The wordmark carries in Rail Navy ink.

Density, type scale, radii, spacing and the agent-state grammar are untouched. Nothing here is a layout change. The tray column is still engraved and still outside the issue-color channel; the working row still reads calm blue; stillness still means "needs you."

Execution

What this costs

With the navy top bar dropped, this is entirely a color change — no layout, no components, no motion. Two of the five rows are mechanical; the last is the only unknown.

index.css
[data-theme="superade"] Replace the derived light block with the table above. Self-contained — one block, no call sites.
index.css
:root shadow tokens Move --shadow-engraved, --shadow-engraved-bar, --shadow-bar-drop and --shadow-popover out of the preset-constant :root into each theme scope. Mechanical, touches both themes — dark keeps its exact current values.
index.css
issue-mix-* call sites Every tinted surface needs its light-mode percentage. The issue-mix-slate-* pattern already establishes how a variant overrides by specificity, so the same mechanism carries a light pair — no new architecture. The largest diff by line count.
styles.css
--work-line-carve Already forked per theme (0.16 / 0.45) — the only place carving was handled correctly. Re-tune to the ink value for consistency.
hardcoded rgb(0 0 0 / …) Anywhere else in the app that assumed a dark ground. Needs a sweep — the count isn't known yet, and it's the one place this could run longer than expected.
DESIGN.md Two amendments: primary in light is Superade Yellow, not Royal Blue; and the Carved Rule gains its light-mode form — compressed ramp, ink grooves, hairlines carrying separation, still no rest shadows.
