# POD-4747 — Two-axis growth test: history ×10, active work ×4

**Question.** Round three's scale target is tens of thousands of issues (operator decision I2,
`docs/decisions/pod-4545-round-three-mobx-linear-review.md` §4.4). A workspace grows along two
axes that cost differently: HISTORY (closed, archived and deleted work and its sessions, past
every visibility window) grows forever; ACTIVE work (open issues, live sessions, visible rows,
worktree lanes) grows with usage. G6 held cold start and heap to 1.1× the legacy control, a ratio
the operator rejected as arbitrary (I4). This issue builds the two axes into the test data and
replaces G6 with a growth test that judges each arm against itself.

**Answer (as of `d9a6be7c2`, flatblock).** **MobX fails the history axis, as expected, and this is its
baseline for the deferred memory cutoff.** At history ×10 (27,601 issues, same 732 visible rows)
its own retained heap goes 40.0 → 160.9 MB (×4.0) and its cold start 652 → 2,405 ms (×3.7), both
far outside the spread of repeated rounds (0.02 MB, 493 ms). Its per-change walls above the
floor stay flat on both axes, and active ×4 grows its heap and cold start sub-linearly (×2.4,
×1.8). The legacy control fails everything on history (walls ~130 ms → ~2.2 s) and its walls
grow ~8× with active ×4. The no-op floor's own build is flat (0.77 MB, ~112 ms at every cell),
and the `hold:1` plant (one object per known issue) fails history (0.91 → 1.36 MB). Below every
arm, the sync engine grows with history by spec: the booted page 21.1 → 96.7 MB, its boot
2.0 → 6.4 s, and the kernel's write-to-feed floor 16 → 96 ms per heartbeat.

## 2. The growth test (replaces G6)

`harness/browser/growth.ts` over a `matrix.ts --cells` directory. Per arm:

| Check | Cells | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| history flat | `h1a1` → `h10a1` | retained heap (coldBootstrap `heapAfter − heapBefore`) and cold start (coldBootstrap `actionMs`) grow by no more than the tolerance |
| active linear | `h1a1` → `h1a4` | the same at `h1a4` ≤ 4 × base + tolerance |
| walls flat | `h1a1` → `h1a4` (and `h10a1`) | each hot-path wall, as the arm's per-round median minus the no-op floor's, grows by no more than the tolerance; the floor's own wall is the engine's and reported |
| switch vs cold (reported) | each cell | principalSwitch `heapAfter` against coldBootstrap `heapAfter`; never an arm failure (the pages differ by more than the arm; the leak gate is the driver's survivor check) |

**Tolerance, from repeated runs.** The larger spread (max − min) of the per-round medians of the
two series a check compares: two cells are flat when they differ by less than one cell differs
from itself across the matrix's four rounds. No constant is typed in. The booted page before
any arm is reported as `engine` (it grows with history by spec until the memory cutoff exists).

**Proven able to fail.** `growth.test.ts` (8 tests) plants each failing shape on synthetic
matrices: a heap that grows 10.1 → 60.1 MB fails history (`GROWS`, exit 1); the `hold:1`
shape (+1 MB at history ×10) fails; growth inside the round spread passes and past it fails; a
heap at 4.5× fails active linear; an arm's wall above the floor that grows fails while a floor
that slows alone does not charge the arm; a switch that keeps 8 MB is marked; a plant that passes
prints `PLANT NOT CAUGHT`. On the real matrix, `noop+hold:1` (one object per known issue,
`harness/web/noop-arm.tsx`) runs beside the arms: **caught**: its retained heap goes 0.91 → 1.36 MB at history ×10 with a
round spread of 0.00 MB (`GROWS`), while the plain floor stays at 0.77 MB (`flat`); at active ×4
it is 1.05 MB (`linear`). Its cold start (125 → 152 ms) stays within the floor's round spread
(41.8 ms): 23,000 small objects cost ~27 ms, less than the page's own noise, which is why the
heap, not the wall, is the check that sees one object per known issue.

**Removed from `summarize.ts`:** G6's lifecycle verdicts (cold bootstrap and retained heap ≤ 1.1 ×
control, switch ≤ 2 ×, growth ≤ control's + 0.05). It reports lifecycle and judges none of it, and
refuses a `--cells` directory.

## 3. Memory by layer
