# POD-4707 (Hc3 follow-up): hand-rolled pool lifecycle walls with lazy row construction at 1x

L5e's lifecycle scenarios for the hand-rolled pool arm with lazy filing and
visibility state (POD-4707) against the same control, at 1x: cold bootstrap
to first painted list, principal switch over a fresh replica, rescope 2x then
back, retained heap after forced GC. Epic POD-4545; coordinator POD-4286. The
question from POD-4588 (Hc3): can the hand arm meet the lifecycle budgets
when per-row filing state is built only for issues a derivation actually
reads, the way the MobX arm's POD-4705 does — or was the O(known issues)
construction intrinsic?

Corpus: `buildCorpus(scale, FIXTURE_SEED)` (L2d live-shaped fixture): 1x 4,867
issues, 4,304 sessions, 468 lanes, 732 visible rows. Feed: `overlaid`.

## 4. Correctness gaps found by the gates, all fixed, all with regression cover

- Evict + re-add of a cold row lost its formal filing: the re-added row
  lands cold (registry, no table write), which the first cut treated as
  a gated candidate instead of a named row, so it never filed and its
  ancestors' progress missed it (truth gate seed 3 step 22: i2351 6/13
  vs 7/14; shrunk to evict + re-add i2516). Fix: every touched known
  issue joins unconditionally, resident or cold — a cold row's filings
  self-heal edge moves through their own cells, while gating would
  leave re-added rows unfiled and reparented ones stale. Covered by the
  truth gate itself (20x300 green after).
- Roll-up nodes that materialised on first read without ever being
  filed leaked on removal: `unfile` only dropped nodes of filed ids
  (eager construction filed every known issue, so every node was a
  filed one). Fix: `sync` unfiles every unknown id it is handed, and
  `syncReplace` drops nodes outside the closure. Covered by
  `pool.test.tsx` ("removes a row with its cells…", which names every
  index entry still pointing at the removed row).

Gates green at the timed SHA: read gate, counts (shared fence budgets),
visible + R-VIS plants, pool, scaling (1x + 4x), bootstrap,
construction (+ cold-heartbeat plant), first-paint order, residency,
relations, rollup + rollup gate, groups, write edit/settle/with-edits,
`gate.test.ts` (3x200), and the hand truth gate **20x300 in four
flatblock chunks (1-5, 6-10, 11-15, 16-20), all green**.

## 5. What the experiment changed

`arms/hand/pool/pool.ts`: the commit's filing step follows the lazy
closure. A `replace` computes it in one plain pass over every known
issue (`plainScope`: the same `VISIBLE_RULES` through
`directVisibleParts`, relations from the raw engine, rows from the raw
tables with cold rows peeked exactly as the live doors peek them, one
row read per id per pass) and files/admits exactly it, dropping held
outsiders. An update files the closure of what it touched (every named
issue plus the session- and worktree-linked candidates the plain pass
keeps: present, keeping, or filed under a held parent) and unfiles what
left; expansion is ancestor chains plus formal subtrees over `Set`s,
never a corpus walk. `worklist/rollup.ts`: `syncReplace` (exact closure
at a replace) and unconditional unfile of unknown rows. The write layer
(`write/edit.ts`) ensures a touched row's closure before it paints its
overlay, since only a derivation reads an overlay.

## 6. Conclusion (decision input)

Lazy construction moves every construction-proportional number by
roughly the closure ratio (2,112/4,867 = 43%): bootstrap cells −47%,
filings −57%, retained heap −23%, switch wall −7%. Steady-state
per-change work is unchanged. **The lifecycle budgets still miss except
the switch pair**: coldBootstrap 2.27x (budget 1.1x), retained heap
2.62x (budget 1.1x), rescope growth 1.321 (budget 1.230); switch wall
1.53x and both switch budgets within. The remainder is the
per-visible-row derivation cost the design keeps (member/visible/part
cells for 2,112 rows, own/rank/placement for 732, plus the view cells
of every mounted row) plus the relation/table substrate for every known
row and the plain pass over the corpus at bootstrap. Making filings lazy answers the
experiment's question the way POD-4705 did: even at 43% construction
the budgets do not come within reach, and the remaining gap is
per-kept-row derivation cost plus substrate, not eager construction.
Per the G6 gate reading, the three misses stay with this mechanism.
